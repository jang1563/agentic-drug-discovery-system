# Cluster-superpopulation sampling-frame calibration

## Status

This is a public synthetic known-truth study. It extends the fixed-profile
informative-cluster-size analysis in `docs/37_informative_cluster_size_estimands.md`.
It contains no real clinical outcomes and makes no treatment, safety, efficacy,
transportability, or regulatory claim.

The implementation is
`agentic_drug_discovery/clinical_outcome_cluster_superpopulation.py`. The protocol binds
the exact stress protocol, fixed-profile protocol, and fixed-profile report by SHA-256
fingerprint before any superpopulation simulation can run.

## Research question

The fixed-profile study held each cluster size and size-linked mean prevalence constant
across replicates. Under informative heterogeneity, its jackknife standard errors were
overconservative relative to repeated outcomes conditional on those fixed clusters.

This study asks whether that discrepancy is primarily a mismatch between:

1. conditional repeated-outcome variation for one fixed set of clusters; and
2. cluster-superpopulation variation when new clusters, with their sizes and mean risks,
   are sampled in every replicate.

The preregistered hypothesis was that the null profiles would remain calibrated while
the informative unequal-size profiles would move from SE-to-empirical-SD ratios near
`1.43-1.59` toward `1.0` after adding cluster-sampling variance. The dominant-cluster
scenario was retained as a stress test, not expected to become operationally eligible.

## Sampling model

For a source scenario with `G` fixed cluster templates, template `t` contains:

- cluster size `M_t`;
- prediction-stratum counts;
- favorable prevalence `p_t`.

Each replicate samples `G` template indices independently and uniformly with replacement:

```text
T_ig ~ Uniform({1, ..., G}),  i = 1, ..., G.
```

Size, stratum layout, and favorable prevalence remain jointly attached to the sampled
template. Outcomes are then generated by the source beta-binomial dependence model and
evaluability is generated by the source outcome-dependent missingness model. Template
sampling, outcomes, and evaluability use separate deterministic RNG substreams.

This is a finite empirical sampling frame. It is deliberately narrower than claiming
that the templates represent a new hospital, trial portfolio, or clinical population.

## Estimands

Let `theta_t(delta)` be the template-specific pattern-mixture functional at log-IMOR
shift `delta`. Under uniform empirical-template sampling, the cluster-balanced target is:

```text
theta_C^SP(delta) = E_T[theta_T(delta)]
                  = (1/G) sum_t theta_t(delta).
```

The unit-weighted target is:

```text
theta_U^SP(delta) = E_T[M_T theta_T(delta)] / E_T[M_T]
                  = sum_t M_t theta_t(delta) / sum_t M_t.
```

These are exactly the fixed-profile known truths. The study therefore changes the
sampling variance while preserving the estimand value. All `600` conditional comparison
cells verify a truth difference of exactly `0.0`.

The distinction between a typical observation and a typical observation from a typical
cluster follows the informative-cluster-size literature. Related primary treatments include
[marginal inference with informative cluster size](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4394393/),
[variance estimation for clustered categorical data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11220780/),
and the
[participant- versus cluster-average estimand discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9908044/).
A
[two-stage superpopulation formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08356)
defines random cluster sizes and explicit size-weighted versus equally weighted effects.

## Methods

The study preserves all three fixed-profile methods and their declared estimands:

| Method | Target | Resampling variance |
|---|---|---|
| `unit_weighted_delete_one_student_t` | Unit weighted | Delete-one cluster jackknife |
| `unit_weighted_delete_mj_student_t` | Unit weighted | Unequal-size delete-mj jackknife |
| `cluster_balanced_delete_one_student_t` | Cluster balanced | Delete-one cluster jackknife |

Each method is evaluated for eight metrics over five log-IMOR values in five scenarios,
with `500` replicates per cell. Bias, interval yield, coverage, and SE calibration retain
the fixed-profile thresholds. No replicate is excluded or reweighted based on realized
dominance, support, direction, coverage, or calibration.

The unit-weighted estimator has a random denominator under this design. Its finite-`G`
bias is measured directly rather than assumed away.

## Results

### Aggregate calibration

| Target | Fixed profile | Cluster superpopulation | Recovered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SE calibration | 280 / 600 | 600 / 600 | 320 |
| All calibration targets | 280 / 600 | 520 / 600 | 240 |

The sampling-frame hypothesis was supported for SE calibration: every one of the `320`
fixed-profile SE failures passed under empirical-template cluster resampling. This did not
make every method valid. The dominant informative scenario retained `80` full-calibration
failures, concentrated in the two unit-weighted methods because coverage remained poor;
the cluster-balanced method passed all `40` of its cells in that scenario.

### Realized design diagnostics

| Scenario | Mean unique templates / 12 | Dominant-cluster rate | Production-eligible rate | P95 max cluster fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| balanced null | 7.754 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.083 |
| dominant informative benefit | 7.740 | 0.628 | 0.372 | 0.267 |
| unequal informative benefit | 7.804 | 0.012 | 0.988 | 0.143 |
| unequal informative harm | 7.720 | 0.018 | 0.982 | 0.143 |
| unequal null | 7.774 | 0.016 | 0.984 | 0.143 |

The dominant scenario demonstrates why a calibrated SE ratio alone is insufficient. Its
unit-weighted methods reached nominal SE scaling while still showing low coverage and
finite-cluster ratio bias. Realized production eligibility remains a diagnostic rate and
never changes the inference denominator.

## Influence diagnostics

The largest cluster can change or tie after template resampling. Diagnostics therefore:

- report the rate at which a unique largest cluster exists;
- condition largest-cluster influence and direction-flip rates on those replicates only;
- use leave-one-out shifts for delete-one methods;
- use pseudovalue contributions for delete-mj, where deletion direction flips are not
  applicable;
- retain only aggregate mean, P95, share, and event-rate summaries.

Influence scales are method-specific concentration diagnostics. Their absolute magnitudes
must not be compared across delete-one and delete-mj methods.

## Interpretation

The study supports a specific explanation, not a universal endorsement of one variance
estimator:

1. The fixed-profile overconservatism for the unequal informative profiles was largely a
   conditional-versus-superpopulation variance mismatch.
2. Null-profile behavior remained stable, arguing against a generic variance inflation
   artifact from template resampling.
3. Severe size-risk concentration can produce finite-cluster bias and undercoverage even
   when the SE/SD ratio is near one.
4. Estimand declaration, dominance controls, bias, yield, coverage, and SE calibration
   must remain separate gates.

The empirical-template model does not estimate uncertainty about the template support
itself. A future external sampling-frame study would need prospectively justified cluster
covariates, transport assumptions, and data not present here.
